# Inspection findings: what we can say about each finding

The only claims the routine may put in "Why it matters" (decisions.json
`why`). Each one names its source. **Status: approved by Daniel, Nov 6,
2026.** New claims still need a reliable source and his OK.
Don't add a claim without a reliable source (CDC, a university extension
service, NPMA).

## Termites (signs of termites, wood touching the ground)

- Termites cause an estimated $5 billion in property damage in the US each
  year. (NPMA)
- Homeowners insurance usually doesn't cover termite damage. (NPMA;
  check the wording against a state insurance department page too)
- Subterranean termites are the termites in western North Carolina; they
  live in the soil and reach wood that touches the ground or stays damp.
  (NC State Extension)
- Wood or siding touching the soil gives termites a hidden way into the
  house. (NC State Extension)

## Rodent droppings, exclusion needed

- Mouse and rat droppings and urine can carry diseases such as hantavirus,
  salmonella, leptospirosis and LCMV. (CDC)
- Don't sweep or vacuum droppings dry: it can stir particles into the air.
  The CDC says to wet them with disinfectant first. (CDC)
- Rodents chew on wiring, which is a fire risk. Do not use a "X% of house
  fires" statistic (no reliable source).
- A mouse can get through a gap about the width of a pencil. (university
  extension; confirm the source)

## Carpenter bees

- Females bore round, about half-inch holes and tunnel along the wood;
  tunnels are usually 6-10 inches in the first year and are extended year
  after year. (university extension)
- Woodpeckers dig into the wood after the larvae, which does more damage.
  (university extension)

## Wasps, hornets, yellow jackets

- A yellow jacket nest grows to thousands of workers over a summer.
  (NC State Extension)
- Old nests aren't reused, but queens favor the same kinds of spots year
  after year. (NC State Extension)
- About 60 people a year die from hornet, wasp and bee stings in the US.
  (CDC)

## Moles, voles, groundhogs (yard burrows)

- Moles mostly live alone; voles live in colonies. (university extension)
- Don't claim foundation damage from moles or voles.
